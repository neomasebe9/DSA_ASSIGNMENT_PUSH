CMPG 224 - Software Engineering

Information Management System (IMS)

Team Project Brief - 2026

Team Size: 5 members  |  Total Marks: 100  |  Duration: 9 Weeks

1. Project Description

The Information Management System (IMS) project requires teams to design, develop, and document a comprehensive software system for managing and organising data within a chosen domain. The system will serve as a central repository for collecting, storing, retrieving, and analysing domain-specific information to improve data accessibility and support informed decision-making.

Teams must clearly define their chosen domain in the project proposal and justify its relevance. The domain must be selected from the approved project topics list in Section 11 or proposed independently, subject to lecturer approval. Independent topic proposals must be submitted for approval no later than Week 2.

Important: Any submission that reuses a project from a previous academic year will receive a mark of zero. No more than one group may work on the same project topic.

2. Objectives

The IMS project must achieve the following objectives:

  1. Develop a functional system capable of reliably managing at least 500 structured records.
  2. Design an intuitive user interface that supports efficient data entry, retrieval, and reporting.
  3. Implement data validation and security mechanisms to ensure data integrity and confidentiality.
  4. Provide reporting and analytics capabilities to support informed decision-making.
  5. Incorporate role-based access control to manage user permissions and data visibility.
  6. Ensure maintainability and modular design to support future updates or enhancements.

3. Scope

The IMS will focus on managing domain-specific data. Core functions include data storage, retrieval, access control, and reporting. The following are explicitly excluded from scope:

  • Hardware infrastructure setup
  • Third-party system integration (e.g., payment gateways, biometric systems)
  • Large-scale deployment or production hosting

4. AI Tool Policy

AI-assisted development tools (e.g., GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude) are permitted in this project. However, their use must be disclosed and documented. Teams are expected to demonstrate understanding of all submitted work — AI-generated code, designs, or documents that a team cannot explain will not receive marks.

Each deliverable must include a brief AI Usage Declaration stating:

  • Which tools were used and for what purpose
  • What the team accepted, modified, or rejected from AI output and why
  • How the team verified that AI-generated content was correct and appropriate

Note: The ability to critically evaluate AI output is itself an assessed skill in this module. Teams that use AI tools thoughtfully and document their reasoning will be rewarded. Teams that submit AI output without engagement will not.

5. Individual vs Team Marks

Most deliverables are assessed as team submissions. However, part of your mark depends on individual performance:

  • The Presentation and Viva (Week 9) includes 4 marks awarded individually - each team member must be able to explain their own contributions and defend the team's decisions.
  • GitHub commit history is reviewed individually - a member with no commits or a single large commit near the deadline may have their implementation mark adjusted.
  • Peer contribution logs are required; if the team cannot demonstrate balanced contributions, marks may be adjusted for each member at the lecturer's discretion.

Note: A team mark reflects what the team produced. An individual mark reflects what you personally understand and contributed. Both matter.

6. Submission Policy

6.1 Late Submissions

No late submissions are accepted. A deliverable not submitted by the deadline receives a mark of zero. No extensions will be granted except in cases of documented medical emergency submitted to the faculty office before the deadline.

6.2 Minimum Functionality Requirement

To receive marks for the system implementation, the submitted system must demonstrably implement at least three core functional requirements from the SRS. A system that only shows a login screen or static interface does not meet this threshold and will receive zero for implementation.

6.3 Missing Team Member Policy

If a team member withdraws from the module during the project, the remaining team must notify the lecturer immediately. The scope may be adjusted at the lecturer's discretion. Remaining members will not be penalised for a withdrawn member's contributions, provided the withdrawal is reported promptly.

7. Requirements

7.1 Functional Requirements

At minimum, the system must support:

  • User registration, authentication, and session management
  • Data entry, editing, deletion, and retrieval
  • Search functionality using multiple filtering criteria
  • Role-based access control (e.g., administrator, staff, manager)
  • Report generation - summaries and performance indicators
  • Data import/export in standard formats (e.g., CSV, PDF)

7.2 Non-Functional Requirements

Quality Attribute

Requirement

Performance

System must handle queries within acceptable response times with 500+ records.

Usability

Interface must be intuitive, with clear navigation and meaningful user feedback.

Security

Sensitive data must be encrypted; access control policies must be enforced.

Scalability

System must accommodate increasing data volume and user load.

Reliability

System must maintain data accuracy and availability under normal operating conditions.

7.3 Requirements Engineering

Teams must identify requirements using appropriate elicitation techniques such as stakeholder interviews, use case modelling, surveys, or observation. All requirements must be documented in an SRS, reviewed by the team, and submitted as a formal deliverable.

Assessor note: Evidence of elicitation (e.g., interview notes, survey results) must be included as an appendix to the SRS. Requirements submitted without elicitation evidence will lose marks.

8. Design

The system architecture must adopt a recognised structural pattern such as MVC or a multi-tier approach. Design considerations must include:

  • Database: Use normalisation and indexing for efficient data management
  • UI/UX: Follow standard usability principles, Nielsen's 10 heuristics as a baseline
  • Modularity: Maintain separation of concerns to support code reuse and testability

The SDD must include class diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs), UI mockups or wireframes, and design rationale for key decisions.

9. Implementation

The system may be developed as a web or desktop application using any appropriate technology stack (e.g., Python/Django, Java/Spring, C#/.NET, PHP/Laravel). Teams must:

  • Justify their technology stack choice in the SDD
  • Follow consistent coding standards and comment code appropriately
  • Use GitHub for version control from Week 2, not only at submission
  • Maintain a commit history that reflects each member's progressive contributions

Important: GitHub commit history will be reviewed at the viva. A single large commit near the deadline raises authenticity concerns and may result in individual mark adjustment.

10. Testing

A structured test plan must be prepared before implementation begins and must include:

  • Unit, integration, and system testing
  • Usability testing with at least two users outside the team
  • Security and performance tests where applicable

All test cases must be traceable to specific SRS requirements. Test results must be documented and submitted alongside the test plan.

11. Collaboration and Communication

Effective teamwork is expected and assessed. Teams must:

  • Use a shared platform (e.g., GitHub, Google Drive, Slack) for communication and document management
  • Keep records of team meetings: date, attendees, decisions, and action items
  • Document individual contributions clearly throughout the project
  • Schedule and attend fortnightly check-ins with the supervisor; arrange these in Week 2

Note: Meeting records and contribution logs must be included as appendices in the final report. Teams that cannot demonstrate individual contributions risk individual mark adjustment.

14. Academic Integrity

The following rules apply to all project submissions:

  • Reuse of projects from previous academic years, in whole or in part, results in a mark of zero for all team members
  • No more than one group may select the same project topic
  • All AI tool usage must be disclosed in the AI Usage Declaration included with each deliverable
  • Individual viva questions verify that each team member understands and can defend all submitted work
  • GitHub commit history is reviewed; absence of progressive commits is grounds for individual mark adjustment
  • No late submissions are accepted under any circumstances except documented medical emergencies

Reminder: Submitting work you do not understand, whether generated by AI or produced by a teammate, is a form of academic dishonesty. Every team member is responsible for understanding the full project.
